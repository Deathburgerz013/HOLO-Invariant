## Core AGI Definition (Established Consensus)
Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) is a hypothetical type of artificial intelligence that matches or surpasses human capabilities across virtually all cognitive tasks. It involves systems capable of understanding, learning, reasoning, applying knowledge, generalizing, and transferring skills across a wide range of domains without task-specific reprogramming or training for each. Key traits include adaptability, autonomous skill acquisition, broad generalization, and transfer learning.

This definition aligns with the broad research consensus summarized in sources such as:
- [Wikipedia – Artificial general intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_general_intelligence)
- [Stanford HAI – What is AGI?](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-definitions/what-is-agi-artificial-general-intelligence)
- IBM, arXiv surveys, Shane Legg, Ben Goertzel, Nick Bostrom, and DeepMind frameworks (performance levels progressing from emerging → competent → expert → virtuoso → superhuman).
