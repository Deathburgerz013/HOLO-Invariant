### Expanded Timeline of Stacked Invariants (Deeper Prehistory to Mid-20th Century Foundational Close)
| }===========================================|
**Deeper Prehistory & Ancient Foundations (~1M+ years ago – ~500 BC)**  
- Controlled fire (combustion): Opportunistic use ~1M+ years ago (Homo erectus evidence); deliberate fire-making ~400,000 years ago (Neanderthal sites, e.g., East Farm/Barnham, England – flint + pyrite sparks, heat-altered sediments/handaxes, repeated hearths). Widespread controlled use ~8000+ BC. **Utility:** Cooking (increased nutrient bioavailability), heating, material transformation (pottery, bricks) — foundational controlled chemical reaction.  
- Fermentation/ethanol & early biochemistry: Evidence ~7000–5000 BC (beer/wine production); possible earlier fish fermentation. **Utility:** Food preservation, early enzymatic process insights.  
- Mineral processing & pigments: Ochre/paints ~100,000 years ago (e.g., Blombos Cave); early soap from fats/ashes. **Utility:** Practical material modification, symbolic use.  
- Metallurgy: Native copper working ~9000–5000 BC; possible early smelting ~10,800+ years ago; bronze alloying (tin + copper) ~3500 BC; iron smelting ~1200 BC (higher temperature challenges). Glass, pottery, dyes. **Utility:** Tools, trade, durable materials.  
- Democritus (~440 BC): Atomism — matter as indivisible particles (verified precursor concept).
| }==========================================|
**Alchemy to Systematic Experiment (~300 BC – 1600 AD)**  
- Aristotle: Four elements framework (earth, air, fire, water) — later challenged empirically.  
- Islamic scholars (Jabir ibn Hayyan, ~8th–9th cent.): Distillation apparatus, acids, experimental methods; systematic classification. **Utility:** Reproducible lab techniques.  
- Paracelsus: Iatrochemistry — chemistry applied to medicine.  
- Robert Boyle (1661): "The Sceptical Chymist" — elements as primitive bodies; experimental rigor; gas behavior precursors. **Utility:** Shift to empirical testing over authority.
| }==========================================|
**Chemical Revolution (17th–18th Centuries)**  
- Joseph Priestley: Isolation of oxygen and other gases.  
- Antoine Lavoisier: Conservation of mass; oxygen role in combustion; overthrow of phlogiston theory. Quantitative chemistry established. **Utility:** Foundational law for stoichiometry, reaction balancing, closed-system precision. Key invariants: precise measurement, mass conservation.
| }==========================================|
**19th Century – Atomic & Structural Foundations**  
- John Dalton (1808): Atomic theory; law of multiple proportions; fixed combining ratios.  
- Friedrich Wöhler (1828): Urea synthesis from inorganic precursors — vitalism disproven. **Utility:** Organic chemistry as part of natural science.  
- Amedeo Avogadro: Hypothesis on gas volumes and number of particles (foundational for mole concept).  
- Dmitri Mendeleev (1869): Periodic table with predictive power (gaps for undiscovered elements). **Utility:** Organizes elements, predicts properties/reactions/trends.  
- Organic advances: Kekulé benzene structure (1865) — structural theory; Berzelius notation.  
- Additional: Laws of chemical dynamics, osmotic pressure (van’t Hoff groundwork).
| }==========================================|
**Early 20th Century – Quantum/Nuclear/Industrial (to mid-20th century foundational close)**  
- J.J. Thomson: Electron discovery; atomic structure advances.  
- Marie & Pierre Curie: Radioactivity; isolation of polonium/radium.  
- Ernest Rutherford: Nuclear model of the atom.  
- Niels Bohr (1913): Atomic model with quantized orbits.  
- James Chadwick (1932): Neutron discovery.  
- Gilbert N. Lewis: Octet rule; valence bond theory foundations.  
- Haber-Bosch process (Fritz Haber, 1918 Nobel): Ammonia synthesis (N₂ + H₂ under high pressure/temperature with catalyst). **Utility:** Industrial fertilizer production; supports global agriculture and population growth (estimated to underpin food for nearly half the world's population).  
- Polymer chemistry: Hermann Staudinger (1920s; 1953 Nobel) — macromolecules concept. Bakelite (1907) as early synthetic plastic. **Utility:** Modern materials science, plastics.  
- Biochemistry crossovers: Fermentation enzymes; early structure determinations (e.g., insulin groundwork). Selective forward bridge: Watson-Crick DNA structure (1953) as foundational biochemistry invariant (enables molecular biology prediction).  
- Nobel linkages (verified): van’t Hoff (1901, physical chemistry), Curie (radioactivity), Haber (1918), etc. Quantum chemistry and thermodynamics principles integrated.
