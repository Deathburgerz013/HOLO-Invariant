1770s: Scheele, Priestley, Lavoisier advance gas chemistry. Lavoisier decomposes/recombines water (H₂O, H+O), establishes conservation of mass, oxygen theory (disproves phlogiston). HCl composition clarified; ammonia (NH₃) studied.  
1805: Gay-Lussac confirms water 2:1 H:O by volume.  
Proust (1797–1806) establishes law of definite proportions via extensive analyses of binary inorganic compounds (oxides, sulfides, sulfates).

Meaning/Invariant: Shift to elements vs. compounds; binaries as tools for mass laws, quantitative analysis, and fixed proportions. Objective reality observable/measurable. 1807–1808: Humphry Davy uses electrolysis to isolate alkali/alkaline earth metals; forms binaries (NaCl, CaO).  
1810s–1820s: Jöns Jacob Berzelius develops chemical notation/symbols, dualistic (electrochemical) theory, accurate atomic weights (determined extensively via binary compounds), and standardized binary nomenclature (-ide suffix).  
1828: Friedrich Wöhler synthesizes urea (CO(NH₂)₂) from inorganic precursors — bridges inorganic/organic; isomerism (e.g., cyanates with Liebig/Berzelius).  
Mid-1800s: Nomenclature standardization (binary ionic: metal nonmetal-ide). Covalent binaries (CO, CO₂, NH₃, HCl) fully characterized. Valence and periodic trends emerge.
